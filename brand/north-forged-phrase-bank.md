## POSITIONING

**The gap:** Detroit owns defiant Michigan. The North owns cozy Michigan (sunsets, pines, "say ya to da U.P."). Nobody owns *defiant North* — the voice of the place that's colder, harder, more remote, and quietly superior.

**Brand promise:** Merch for people who don't just visit winter — they win it. The North as combatant, not postcard.

**Voice:** Deadpan, granite-dry, understated menace. Never whiny, never try-hard. The lake doesn't yell; neither do we. Think Raygun's wit crossed with a Carhartt work ethic, spoken through a snowbank.

**Audience:**
- Year-round locals (Yoopers, NoMi lifers) who roll their eyes at fudgie souvenir tees
- Expats/diaspora who want to flex their origin story from Charlotte or Denver
- Seasonal people who *aspire* to local status (they'll buy it to prove they get the joke)

**Core tension to mine:** Yoopers vs. trolls. Locals vs. fudgies/downstaters. The North vs. everyone who thinks "Up North" means their cottage. Winter as a filter, not a season.

**Differentiation:** Everyone else sells the North as *pretty*. We sell it as *proving ground*.

**Product lanes:** Heavyweight tees, midweight hoodies, beanies, ball caps, stickers/decals, camp mugs. Muted palette — iron gray, spruce, rust, bone, blaze orange accents. Blocky slab-serif or stencil type. No script fonts, no sunsets.

**⚠️ Trademark cautions (verify before printing):**
- "Yooper Shirts" is a registered mark (Marquette). "Yooper" alone is widely used but check current status.
- The M-22 logo is trademarked — avoid entirely.
- "Vs Everybody" — avoid per your call; the format is litigious territory anyway.
- "Superior"-based names: strong option, but run clearance on apparel classes first.
- Avoid Gordon Lightfoot lyric phrasing ("never gives up her dead").
- New in v2: avoid naming real workwear brands on product; "sisu," "löyly," and "pasty" are generic/cultural terms, not marks, but keep usage respectful and non-parodic.

**How to read this bank:** Every section now runs 1–50. Entries marked ★ are from the original 100 and keep their tested phrasing. Everything else is new. Sections are lanes, not silos — a Weather line works on a beanie, a Closer works on a hoodie back.

## PRODUCTION NOTES (v2)
- **Tees (heavyweight):** Sections I, III, XI — long lines, chest or full back, stencil caps.
- **Hoodies:** VI, VII, XI — the "earned" lines; small left-chest hit, big back block.
- **Beanies/caps:** short lines only, 3–5 words. Best sources: I (39, 46, 47), VIII (10, 12, 21), IV (18, 38).
- **Stickers/decals:** V and X carry the most sticker energy — hostility and lore both travel well small.
- **Camp mugs:** II and VIII — the daily-use lines (weather brags, camp rules) land where coffee happens.
- **Avoid pairing** a Section V hostility line with a Section IX pretty-place line on the same SKU; the tones cancel.
